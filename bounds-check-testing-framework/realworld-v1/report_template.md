## What the reviewers asked, and what this answers

All four reviewers scored Experimental Evaluation 0 (`IICT_CONTEXT/02-REVIEWS.md`):

- R1 asked for "a larger, more diverse benchmark suite".
- R2 criticised "one minimal shader + one matmul; no real corpus".
- R3 wrote "it is unclear if the selected patterns cover enough optimisation opportunities".

The v4 kernels were written by this project, and they were also the kernels used to find the gaps that features 005 and 006 close. This benchmark uses only code the project did not write:

- **vello**, the Linebender 2D renderer: the crate published as `vello`, which is `research/vello_research` at `a9f11bb`.
  - Its full compute pipeline: 20 shaders, 16 dispatches per frame.
  - All **34 of vello's own test scenes**.
  - Each frame's work is generated by vello's own recording code, then replayed on the fork.
- **webgpu-samples imageBlur**: the unmodified `blur.wgsl` on the sample's own image, driven like `main.ts`.

## Step 0: which real-world binaries elision changes at all

The screen compiled every entry point of the 98 census files (147 entry points) under wgpu-hal's production Vulkan options (spec 005, `screen-19ffb4e.jsonl`). **28 entry points change their binary.**

- **vello**: 14 of its 18 census entry points; UMin clamps 193 → 93.
- **Everything else is small**:
  - imageBlur: 4 → 1;
  - rerun `rectangle_fs`: 10 → 1, over three entry points;
  - bevy `downsample_depth_first`: 60 → 58.

The benchmark's own vello shader set is slightly larger: 20 shaders, including the setup and scan variants. There, UMin goes **197 → 95**. Eleven of those are `min()` calls written in the shader source; they are still present in the `unchecked` build. So the **bounds clamps go 186 → 84, i.e. 102 removed**.

## Who actually runs these checks

This scope applies to every number below.

- **Native vello turns all runtime checks off.** It creates its shader modules with `create_shader_module_trusted(.., ShaderRuntimeChecks::unchecked())` (vello `research/vello_research/src/wgpu_engine.rs:848-853`). So elision cannot speed up vello as a native app.
- **Web content cannot turn them off.**
  - WebGPU's `GPUShaderModuleDescriptor` has only `code` and `compilationHints` (archived spec).
  - wgpu's browser backend ignores the runtime-check argument (`wgpu/src/backend/webgpu.rs:1829`).
- **So the slots mean:**
  - `no_bce`: vello as web content in a wgpu-based browser today, for example Firefox. Firefox's exact settings are UNKNOWN from this repo.
  - `bce`: the same, with this project's elision.
  - `unchecked`: roughly vello native, with no bounds checks.
- imageBlur is a WebGPU sample, so it is web content by construction.

## Method

The method is the same as v4 except where stated.

**Compilation and submission**

- Passthrough SPIR-V: the driver runs exactly the recorded words.
- wgpu-hal's production Vulkan policy: index Restrict; buffer Unchecked (the robustBufferAccess2 case); force_loop_bounding; native zero-init; SPIR-V 1.6.
- **New:** wgpu-hal's descriptor-binding renumbering. Passthrough needs it whenever a group's bindings are not 0..n-1.

**Measurement**

- 4 slots per round, in rotating order: `no_bce`, `bce`, `bce_dup` (identical words to `bce`, the in-round control), `unchecked`.
- Per-round ratios and a steady-state warm-up.
- Noise gate before **every** run (no other GPU compute, load below 2) and again after.
- Provenance gate and nvidia-smi telemetry.

**Campaign**

- 10 runs per workload on NVIDIA and 5 on Intel. Workload order is reshuffled every cycle.
- A 90 s settling period between the two GPUs.
- The verdict rules and the ±0.5% control margin are v4's.

### vello replay

`realworld-v1/vello-capture` records vello's own per-frame command stream. It calls `low_level::Render::render_encoding_coarse` and then `record_fine`, which are the calls `Renderer::render_to_texture` makes.

The harness replays that stream the way vello's `WgpuEngine::run_recording` executes it:

- buffers sized to vello's size classes, and bound whole;
- one compute pass per dispatch;
- uploads applied first;
- the zero-size-dispatch workaround.

The replay reproduces vello's own output byte for byte. On `blend_grid`, vello's own renderer produces exactly the two images the replay produces.

### Output oracle

- **imageBlur**: outputs must be byte-identical across slots, before and after timing.
- **vello**: vello's own renderer is **not bit-deterministic** on every scene. Over 40 renders per scene:

  | GPU | scenes with one image | scenes with several images |
  |---|---|---|
  | NVIDIA | 28 of 34 | 6 (2 to 40 images each) |
  | Intel | 25 of 33 | 8 (4 to 39 images each) |

  So the vello oracle compares elided outputs with a no-elision baseline: vello's own images on that GPU, plus 40 `no_bce` replays. An elided run may deviate only where the baseline itself deviates, subject to a leave-one-out threshold. This rule was fixed before the campaign.

## Reading the results

1. **Intel iGPU (Mesa): elision recovers essentially the whole cost of the checks.**
   - **Unflagged scenes (27):** elision gain geomean **1.0848 (+8.48%)**; cost of all checks 1.0855; bce/unchecked 1.0006.
   - **All 34 scenes:** +6.71%.
   - **Range:** from **+34.27%** (`image_extend_modes (bilinear)`) down to **−5.30%** (`robust_paths`). 21 scenes are RESOLVED as gains and 5 as losses.
   - **The losses are not elision-specific.** `no_bce/unchecked` is also below 1 on the same scenes, so removing the clamps *at all* makes those scenes slower on this driver.
   - **Controls are tight**, mostly ≤0.3%.
2. **NVIDIA laptop GPU: small.**
   - **Unflagged scenes (30):** gain geomean **1.0027 (+0.27%)**, against a cost of all checks of 1.0134. Elision recovers little of that cost (bce/unchecked 1.0081).
   - **Resolved:** 4 scenes as gains, all around +1% except `many_draw_objects` at **+4.45%**. None as losses.
   - **Why most scenes are unresolvable.** The in-round control deviated by 0.5% or more in some run. vello frames are short (bce median 0.12–8.5 ms per frame) and span many passes. The ±0.5% margin, taken from v4's single-kernel dispatches, is too tight for them on this GPU.
3. **imageBlur: no resolvable effect on either GPU.** Removing one clamp from the hot filter loop is below this harness's resolution: Intel +0.34%, with controls at 0.02%, still under the 0.5% margin.
4. **Different GPUs, different answers**, as in v4. The same 102 removed clamps are worth about 0% on NVIDIA and about 8% on Intel.

## Correctness: the oracle-flagged scenes

**What was flagged.** The pre-declared oracle flagged 4 scenes on NVIDIA (`splash_with_tiger`, `cardioid_and_friends`, and both `longpathdash` scenes). It flagged 7 on Intel: the same 4 plus the three `stroke_styles` scenes. Every flag was a deviation of 1–2 pixels, and the `unchecked` slot, which has no elision, produced such deviations too.

**The test.** Under the spec's "bad" branch, timing interpretation of those scenes was suspended and a **race-location test** was run: 200 interleaved outputs per slot per scene (table above). If elision miscompiled something, elided binaries would deviate, persistently, at pixels where no-elision binaries never deviate.

**The result.**
- Across the 15 valid probes, **8** pixels varied only under elided binaries and **11** only under no-elision binaries.
- Per scene, it's 0–2 either way.
- The rate of runs landing off the modal image is the same in all four slots.
- On `splash_with_tiger` (NVIDIA), the elided binaries were byte-identical to vello's own image in 200 of 200 runs.

That is the signature of vello's own heavy-tailed nondeterminism, sampled finitely (40 baseline renders are too few to catch all of it). It is not the signature of a miscompile.

**How the flags are reported.** The campaign verdicts are left as computed ("oracle-flagged"). The flagged scenes are excluded from the "unflagged" geomeans above and included in the "all 34" ones.

## Findings made while building this

These are recorded in more detail in spec 005, "Bring-up findings".

- **webgpu-samples imageBlur ships a race and an out-of-bounds read.**
  - `main.ts` passes filterDim 16 with blockDim 113.
  - The shader then reads `tile[r][128]`, one past the end of a length-128 array.
  - Neighbouring workgroups write one shared column with different values: all 9,168 differing pixel instances lie within 8 px of a multiple of 113.
  - The elision analysis correctly left that access **unproven**.
  - Not reported upstream; that is the user's decision.
- **wgpu-hal Vulkan renumbers descriptor bindings.** Passthrough SPIR-V has to replicate this, or it reads the wrong descriptors. It was caught as a vacuous all-zero "identical outputs" pass.
- **Native vello runs unchecked, and vello is not bit-deterministic** (both described above).

## Deviations and limits

- **Refused runs.** The harness refused 2 of the 190 Intel runs because the end timestamp came before the start: `blur_4k` in cycle 3 and `vello:26-image_sampling` in cycle 5. Those two workloads have 4 Intel runs each.
- **Disk incident (my error).** The race-location probe first saved every distinct output image, which filled the root disk (28 GB).
  - The images were deleted after their summaries were written.
  - The probe now keeps its pixel sets in memory.
  - One probe result (Intel `stroke_styles (skew)`, image-based) is invalid and was redone.
- **Hardware coverage.** Two GPUs in one laptop, Vulkan only. No Metal or D3D12. Metal also checks buffers, so it may gain more (UNKNOWN).
- **Workload realism.**
  - The vello scenes are vello's own test scenes, not a trace of a shipping app.
  - imageBlur is a sample.
  - The `no_bce` slot models a wgpu-based browser's Vulkan path; Firefox's exact settings are not verified here.
- **NVIDIA resolution.** Control noise makes most NVIDIA scenes "not resolvable" under v4's rules. The rules were not relaxed after seeing the data.
